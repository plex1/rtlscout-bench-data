### Highest-impact optimizations
1. **Gated clocks** (biggest single win): eliminated all enable muxes on the data paths.
2. **Booth radix-4 with pre-decoded weights**: the weight load shift register stores Booth codes, so the per-cycle multiply is just a magnitude mux + XOR-negate + CSA tree â no general multiplier.
3. **Pipeline splitting** of multiply vs. accumulate (with matched deskew reduction), enabled by deriving the exact per-cycle dataflow: `c_data[j](Ï) = Î£_i a_i(Ïâ15)Â·w_ij(Ïâ15+i+j)`.
4. **Width narrowing**: per-depth accumulator widths 16/17/17/18/18/18/18/19 and 19-bit deskew registers (result provably fits in 19 bits; all arithmetic mod 2Â¹â¹ with truncated-carry shifting is exact).
5. **Reset removal** on data arrays (only the 15-bit valid pipe resets) â safe because `c_valid` gates every data comparison and the pipes are structurally flushed.

### What didn't work / regressions
- **Fully carry-save accumulate chain** (`v13_csbooth.v`, 1.72M): no CPA per cycle, but the 7-input compression in the PE was both larger and slower than v12's split.
- **Sklansky prefix adders** (`v15`, `v17`, ~1.83M): abc's default `+` mapping beat my hand-built prefix adders in both area and delay.
- **Folding the Booth correction row into the multiply stage** (`v16`, 1.84M): smaller area but pushed delay up.
- Several outright bugs caught by the vectors: zero-extension via concatenation when narrowing widths, an unused declared signal (`pc2`) that silently dropped a CSA row, Booth correction bits at wrong bit positions, and a wrong Sklansky partner index.

### Lessons
- Deriving the exact cycle-by-cycle dataflow equation first made aggressive retiming/narrowing safe and easy to verify.
- Synthesis tool behavior (abc's adder mapping, clock-gating inference) matters as much as RTL structure â hand-optimized arithmetic often lost to the default mapping.
- Reset removal and clock gating are huge area levers when the protocol guarantees structural flush and the output is valid-gated.
- I'd budget more steps for exploring around v12 (e.g., different Booth groupings, hybrid CSA depths per column, and target_delay sweeps), since several late variants were within ~10% and close variants of the winner often pay off.
